Week-1 
Introduction to Theatre and 
Voice Modulation 
During the first week of the Theatre Ability Enhancement course, participants 
were introduced to the fundamental principles of stage performance, including 
essential theatrical terminology, stage etiquette, and the mindset required for 
effective acting. The instructors highlighted the significance of theatre as a 
medium of communication and its influence on society. Additionally, voice 
modulation was identified as a key performance skill, enabling actors to regulate 
pitch, enhance vocal expression, and effectively convey emotions during 
dialogue delivery. 
Week2 
Introduction to Navarasa 
 
During the second week of the Theatre Ability Enhancement Course, the focus 
shifted towards understanding and applying the concept of Navarasa, a 
fundamental aspect of Indian theatrical tradition. Navarasa comprises nine 
primary emotions that form the emotional foundation of dramatic performance. 
Mastery of these emotions enables actors to portray characters convincingly and 
establish a meaningful connection with the audience. 
The nine rasas explored during the course were: 
• Shringara (Love/Beauty):  Represents love, attraction, and aesthetic 
appreciation, expressed through graceful gestures, gentle facial 
expressions, and a sense of joy. 
• Hasya (Laughter/Comedy): Depicts humor, amusement, and playfulness 
through cheerful behavior, lively expressions, and effective comic timing. 
• Karuna (Compassion/Pathos): Reflects sorrow, empathy, and emotional 
sensitivity, often portrayed through expressions of grief and sympathy. 
• Raudra (Anger/Fury):  Conveys intense anger and aggression through 
powerful vocal delivery, strong body language, and fierce expressions. 
• Veera (Heroism/Courage):  Symbolizes bravery, confidence, and 
determination, demonstrated through commanding posture and assertive 
actions.

• Bhayanaka (Fear/Terror):  Represents fear, anxiety, and suspense, 
expressed through cautious movements and tense facial expressions. 
• Bibhatsa (Disgust/Aversion): Portrays feelings of revulsion and rejection 
through gestures and expressions indicating discomfort or disapproval. 
• Adbhuta (Wonder/Amazement):  Captures curiosity, surprise, and 
fascination, often shown through expressive reactions and gestures of 
awe. 
• Shanta (Peace/Tranquility):  Represents calmness, inner peace, and 
spiritual balance, conveyed through relaxed posture and composed 
expressions. 
The course provided detailed instruction on the meaning, facial expressions, 
body movements, and emotional nuances associated with each rasa. This 
theoretical understanding was reinforced through practical enactments in which 
students performed scenes designed to portray specific emotions. These 
exercises highlighted the importance of body language, vocal modulation, and 
facial expressions in effectively communicating emotions to an audience. 
A distinctive component of the training involved performing transition scenes 
that required students to shift seamlessly between different rasas. Working in 
groups of four to five members, participants enacted assigned scenarios that 
demonstrated emotional transitions within a performance. Subsequently, groups 
developed their own scripts, dialogues, and stage situations, enabling them to 
creatively explore emotional progression and character development. 
 
Theatre Games and Expression Development 
To further strengthen expressive abilities, spontaneity, and observational skills, 
the course incorporated a variety of theatre-based games and activities. 
One of the key exercises was Mirror Acting , where participants worked in 
pairs. One student assumed the role of the leader while the other mirrored their 
movements in real time. This activity enhanced concentration, coordination, 
observation, and non-verbal communication skills, all of which are essential for 
effective stage performance. 
Another engaging activity was a Word Guessing Game , in which participants 
communicated assigned words or phrases solely through actions and 
expressions, without using speech. The remaining members of the group 
attempted to identify the word being portrayed. This exercise encouraged 
creativity and demonstrated the effectiveness of physical expression in 
conveying meaning.

Acting Sessions 
The week also featured several practical acting exercises aimed at developing 
confidence and collaborative performance skills. 
 
Improvised Group Performances 
Participants were divided into small groups consisting of three to four members. 
Each group was required to create and perform a short improvised scene on the 
spot. The activity encouraged creativity, teamwork, and spontaneous thinking 
while providing an inclusive environment free from language constraints. 
 
Navarasa Performance Exercise 
In this activity, each participant was assigned a number and was randomly called 
upon to enact a specific rasa. Students were required to express the assigned 
emotion primarily through facial expressions, gestures, and body language 
rather than spoken dialogue. This exercise emphasized the significance of non -
verbal communication in theatrical performance and provided valuable practical 
experience in emotional expression. 
 
Reflection 
The activities conducted during the second week successfully combined 
creativity, teamwork, and skill development. The theatre games fostered 
interaction and cooperation among participants, while the acting exercises 
encouraged students to move beyond their comfort zones and explore various 
forms of self -expression. The Navarasa activities, particularly those involving 
emotional transitions and non -verbal performance, provided valuable insights 
into the role of emotions in theatre. 
Overall, the week offered a highly engaging and interactive learning experience, 
strengthening participants' understanding of emotional expression and laying a 
solid foundation fo more advanced theatrical training

Week3 
Script Development and Creative Scene Writing 
The third week of the Theatre Ability Enhancement Course focused on the 
fundamentals of scriptwriting and creative scene development. Students were 
introduced to the role of a script as the foundation of any theatrical performance, 
providing a structured framework for dialogue, character interactions, and stage 
action. 
Scriptwriting was presented as the process of creating written narratives for 
various forms of performance, including theatre, film, television, and other 
visual media. A script serves as a blueprint that guides actors, directors, and 
production teams in bringing a story to life. The sessions covered the essential 
components of scriptwriting, including format, plot structure, dialogue 
construction, character development, and action descriptions. Students also 
learned about different types of scripts, such as screenplays, teleplays, stage 
plays, and radio scripts. 
A major practical component of the week involved creative scene writing 
exercises. Instead of working with predefined scripts, students were provided 
with various situations or scenarios and were asked to develop original scenes 
based on them. Working individually or in groups, participants created 
dialogues, characters, and storylines that effectively portrayed the assigned 
situation. This activity encouraged imagination, storytelling ability, and 
collaborative thinking while demonstrating how a simple idea can be 
transformed into a meaningful dramatic performance. 
The script development exercises also emphasized the importance of structuring 
scenes logically, creating engaging character interactions, and ensuring that 
emotions and intentions were clearly communicated through dialogue and 
action. Students were encouraged to think critically about how characters would 
react in different circumstances and how conflicts and resolutions could be 
effectively portrayed within a short performance. 
Following the writing process, groups performed their scenes before the class, 
allowing them to experience the transition from script creation to stage 
presentation. These performances provided valuable opportunities to observe 
different interpretations of the same scenario and to understand the relationship 
between written text and live performance. 
The week concluded with discussions and feedback sessions in which 
instructors evaluated the scripts and performances, offering suggestions on

presentation. Through these activities, students gained a deeper appreciation for 
the creative process behind theatrical production and developed essential skills 
in storytelling, collaboration, and performance preparation. 
Overall, Week 3 provided a practical introduction to scriptwriting and scene 
construction, enabling participants to combine creativity with theatrical 
techniques while strengthening their confidence in both writing and 
performance. 
 
Week 4 
Mime and Non-Verbal Communication 
The fourth week of the Theatre Ability Enhancement Course focused on mime, 
a unique form of theatrical expression that relies entirely on non-verbal 
communication. Mime is the art of conveying stories, emotions, and situations 
through body movements, facial expressions, and gestures without the use of 
spoken language. This form of performance emphasizes the actor’s ability to 
communicate effectively through physical expression alone. 
The sessions introduced the fundamental principles and techniques of mime. 
Students learned that successful mime performances require clarity of 
movement, precise gestures, and strong facial expressions to ensure that the 
audience can easily understand the intended message. Particular emphasis was 
placed on developing focus and concentration, enabling performers to create the 
illusion of interacting with invisible objects, environments, and characters. 
Several key techniques were explored during practical exercises. Facial 
expressions were used to portray a wide range of emotions such as happiness, 
sadness, surprise, and anger. Body movements were carefully practiced to depict 
actions and situations without physical props, including common mime 
techniques such as interacting with an invisible wall, climbing imaginary stairs, 
or pulling a rope. Students also learned the importance of focus, which helps 
direct the audience’s attention toward imagined objects and enhances the 
realism of the performance. 
The course also introduced the general rules and conventions commonly 
associated with mime performances. Participants were required to perform 
without speech, relying solely on physical actions and expressions. The 
importance of maintaining clear and understandable movements was 
emphasized to ensure effective communication with the audience. Discussions

also covered performance guidelines typically followed in mime presentations 
and competitions, including adherence to time limits, appropriate content, and 
minimal reliance on props or external aids. 
Practical sessions provided students with opportunities to create and perform 
short mime sequences individually and in groups. These activities encouraged 
creativity, observation, and precise physical control while demonstrating how 
complex ideas and emotions can be communicated without dialogue. 
Overall, Week 4 enhanced participants' understanding of non-verbal 
communication and highlighted the expressive potential of movement and facial 
expressions in theatrical performance. The mime exercises strengthened 
physical awareness, creativity, and stage presence, contributing significantly to 
the development of essential acting skills. 
 
WEEK 5 
MADADS AND ITS GENERAL DISCUSSION 
The fifth week of the Theatre Ability Enhancement Course focused on a 
practical performance activity inspired by the concept of MADADS (Ad-Mad 
Show). While traditional Ad-Mad events typically involve teams creating and 
presenting original advertisements through skits, jingles, and slogans, the 
activity conducted during the course adopted a different approach that 
emphasized expression and performance skills. 
During the session, a Kannada narration was presented by either senior students 
or members of our batch. Participants were then divided into small groups of 
three members and assigned the task of enacting the narrated content. Since the 
narration served as the voice-over for the performance, students were required to 
rely primarily on facial expressions, gestures, body language, and movement to 
communicate the emotions and actions described in the story. 
This exercise highlighted the importance of non-verbal communication in 
theatre. Participants learned how expressive acting can effectively complement a 
voice-over and enhance audience understanding without the need for spoken 
dialogue. The activity also reinforced concepts previously learned through mime 
and Navarasa exercises, particularly the use of facial expressions and physical 
movements to portray emotions and character intentions. 
Working in small groups encouraged teamwork, coordination, and creative 
interpretation of the narration. Each group developed its own style of

performance, demonstrating different ways of translating the spoken narrative 
into visual action. The exercise required careful observation, timing, and 
synchronization with the voice-over to ensure that the performance remained 
engaging and coherent. 
The feedback session that followed emphasized the role of expression, stage 
presence, and coordination in creating impactful performances. Students 
received guidance on improving clarity of actions, emotional projection, and 
audience engagement. 
Overall, Week 5 provided a valuable opportunity to apply non-verbal acting 
techniques in a collaborative setting. The activity strengthened participants' 
ability to communicate through expression and movement while enhancing their 
confidence, creativity, and performance skills. 
WEEK 6 
STAGE POSITIONING AND ENTRY AND EXIT POINTS 
 
The sixth week of the Theatre Ability Enhancement Course focused on the 
practical aspects of stagecraft, particularly stage positioning, movement, and 
the effective use of performance space. The sessions introduced students to the 
importance of actor placement on stage and how positioning can influence 
audience attention, character significance, and overall scene composition. 
Participants learned about various stage areas, including centre stage, which is 
often used for key moments and important characters, as well as other stage 
regions that contribute to visual balance and storytelling. The concept of wings 
was also explained, highlighting their role as offstage areas used for actor 
preparation, entrances, exits, and scene transitions. 
A significant portion of the training was devoted to understanding stage 
entrances and exits. Students were taught how actors should enter and leave the 
stage naturally and purposefully, ensuring smooth transitions that maintain the 
flow of the performance. The sessions emphasized that movement on stage 
should be deliberate and aligned with the dramatic requirements of the scene. 
The instructors also discussed how actor positioning can reflect relationships 
between characters, indicate authority or importance, and guide audience focus. 
Through demonstrations and examples, students gained an understanding of 
how thoughtful staging enhances both visual appeal and narrative clarity. 
To apply these concepts, participants were divided into groups and assigned 
specific scenarios to perform. Each group was required to plan and execute its 
[PAGE:9] Page | 9 
 
 
scene while carefully considering stage placement, character positioning, 
entrances, exits, and movement patterns. This exercise encouraged students to 
integrate theatrical techniques into their performances and to think critically 
about how space can be used effectively to support storytelling. 
The practical performances provided valuable experience in coordinating 
movement with dramatic action and helped students develop greater awareness 
of stage dynamics. Feedback from instructors focused on improving spatial 
awareness, maintaining audience engagement, and ensuring that stage 
movement contributed meaningfully to the scene. 
Overall, Week 6 enhanced participants' understanding of stage management and 
performance techniques, demonstrating how effective positioning, movement, 
and use of stage space contribute to a well -structured and engaging theatrical 
presentation.
